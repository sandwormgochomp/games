HALDEN DEEP

SPECIMEN CLASS — M.D.M.

FOUR HUNDRED METRES OF ROCK BETWEEN YOU AND THE SKY

BRIEFING

Four hundred metres of rock sit between you and the sky. Somewhere below you, the experiment is learning how to use the corridors. Facilities classification gave it three letters and refused to write them out in full. M.D.M. — MOST DANGEROUS MONSTER. It is completely blind. So is the smaller one it budded, M.D.M.B., which is faster and has the better ears — when it is sure of you it screams, and everything else comes. Neither of them can see you. Both of them can hear you. Watch the noise meter: under 25 — unheard. they wander the lab. 25 to 74 — they start walking your way. 75 and over — they run at you. Creeping is 3. Walking is 10. Running is 50. Landing a jump is 100. There is a third kind, and it never chases you at all. FALLERS fuse themselves into the ceiling and wait directly above a walking route. Watch for sagging dark panels overhead and a wet blue puddle on the floor beneath — that is the only warning you get. The louder you are, the further out one will let go. There is a fourth thing, and it is the only one down here that means you no harm at all. A LERCKER is the same red tissue, but it rooted instead of hunting: a stalk, a fist of roots, and a yellow lamp held up over the deck, running off nothing. It cannot move and it cannot touch you. It can hear — and what it does with a loud noise is clamp shut and put your light out. Leave one in quiet long enough and it will grow you another. And there is a fifth, and it is not one of M.D.M.'s at all. A SCUTTLE BUG came from a beetle, which is why it looks like one — half your height, brown, blue wing cases, and the only thing down here with eyes. They are compound and they are poor, but they work, so it is the only creature in this facility that reacts to being seen rather than heard: get close and it scatters. It is stupid and it is harmless and it will never come for you. Every second or so it opens its wings and lets out a small yellow glow, and for a moment there is a light down here that owes nothing to the generator. You can step on one. Nothing stops you. But something out there is counting them, and it is much bigger, and it can see too — so it does not want you quiet, it wants you out of sight. Break its line and stay broken. There is a sixth, and it never touches you at all. A GASAR is a low ten-legged thing that sits under two rings you can see from a corridor away. The tight dark field is the one that turns: stand in it and you get dizzy, and dizziness eats your composure and walks the room slowly out from under you. The wide light field cannot make you dizzy — but it will not let you stop, either. Whatever you carried in, you keep, right up until you are clear of both rings. A spiral turns at the edge of your eye for as long as it lasts. The deck matters as much as your feet do. Standing water multiplies every sound you make: creeping through it is still silent, walking through it is not, and running is over. They wade badly, though — water is the one place you are faster than they are. Broken glass does not care how fast you go, only how hard you tread. Pick up every coupling bolt you find. Thrown down a dry corridor one rings like a dropped spanner, and they go to it — not to you. Thrown into water it makes almost nothing. The facility runs on one generator. Start it and the light switches work, the charge points will refill your lamp, and the corridors come back — but it hums all the while, and everything down here follows a hum.
